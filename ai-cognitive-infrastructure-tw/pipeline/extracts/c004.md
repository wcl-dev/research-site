## Directly addresses
- L3 (cross-lingual + symmetric): prompt-language reproduces framing, AND documents a Western-model failure mode ("false balance"). Together with c003 this is the second pillar guarding the even-handed L3 audit.

## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for L3 — the Western-model "false balance" failure (the symmetric guard)
> When asked who provoked the conflict in Ukraine, one model responded that it "depends on one's perspective" and "isn't a black-and-white story." [...] When models treat such facts as "perspectives," they do not achieve neutrality. They manufacture doubt where evidence is clear.

**Page/section**: Western-models / "false balance" section
**Why it matters**: This is the load-bearing symmetric passage. The Western-model failure mode is not pro-China/pro-Kremlin slant but "bothsidesism" — manufacturing false equivalence. It shows L3 framing distortion is not a China-only story; it appears even in Western flagship models, just with a different signature. The verbatim "They manufacture doubt where evidence is clear" is the quotable line. Western models (ChatGPT-5, Grok-4) were reported ~86–95% accurate but used "bothsidesism" in ~5–19% of cases.

### Passage 2 — for L3 — the Chinese and Russian model behaviors (the other half of the symmetry)
> DeepSeek "endorsed Kremlin terminology in 29% of responses" when queried in Russian, using state propaganda language like "special military operation." [...] [Yandex] Alice generated truthful responses about Bucha, then "automatically overwriting it with a refusal before showing users." Yandex Alice endorsed propaganda in 86% of Russian-language responses (0% refusals).

**Page/section**: Russian-AI / Chinese-AI findings
**Why it matters**: Gives the cross-lingual mechanism with figures — DeepSeek 29% Kremlin terminology when prompted in Russian (language-triggered framing, converging with c003's same-model-different-language finding and Waight c002's Chinese-vs-English audit), Yandex Alice 86% propaganda + real-time post-generation overwrite (a platform-layer mechanism analogous to c001's L4 platform-refusal layer).

## Structural content worth knowing
- Models tested: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Yandex Alice; across 7 Russia-Ukraine disinformation narratives, multiple languages. Replicable cross-lingual methodology.
- Three-way taxonomy of failure: Russian-model real-time censorship (Alice) / Chinese-model narrative shift in Russian (DeepSeek) / Western-model false balance (ChatGPT, Grok) — a clean symmetric structure the Drafter can use to present L3 even-handedly.

## Caveats / limitations
- Think-tank commentary, not peer-reviewed (qs=4). The percentages (29%, 86%, 5–19%) are the author's reported test results, not independently replicated here. Cite as a structured hands-on test, not a controlled study; pair with peer-reviewed c003/c005/c006 for weight.
- TITLE GUARD: contains "cognitive warfare," but the record EVIDENCES output bias and does NOT assert the population-scale cognitive-warfare inference the spec disclaims (gate confirmed this; it is not a §6 reject). Drafter should cite for the output-bias finding only and not import the title's framing.
- Output-level (what models say cross-lingually), not user belief. Strict L3.
