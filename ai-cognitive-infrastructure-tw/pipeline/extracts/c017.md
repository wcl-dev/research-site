## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for L4a — the monoculture-welfare mechanism (verbatim abstract)
> As algorithms are increasingly applied to screen applicants for high-stakes decisions in employment, lending, and other domains, concerns have been raised about the effects of algorithmic monoculture, in which many decision-makers all rely on the same algorithm. This concern invokes analogies to agriculture, where a monocultural system runs the risk of severe harm from unexpected shocks. Here we show that the dangers of algorithmic monoculture run much deeper, in that monocultural convergence on a single algorithm by a group of decision-making agents, even when the algorithm is more accurate for any one agent in isolation, can reduce the overall quality of the decisions being made by the full collection of agents. Unexpected shocks are therefore not needed to expose the risks of monoculture; it can hurt accuracy even under "normal" operations, and even for algorithms that are more accurate when used by only a single decision-maker. Our results rely on minimal assumptions, and involve the development of a probabilistic framework for analyzing systems that use multiple noisy estimates of a set of alternatives.

**Why it matters**: This is the theoretical priors for L4a. The key transferable idea: monoculture harm "it can hurt ... even under 'normal' operations" — no shock needed. BUT the SCOPE is explicit and load-bearing: "screen applicants for high-stakes decisions in employment, lending" and "decision-making agents" — these are institutions making screening decisions, NOT individuals consuming one model's information framing. The Drafter must present c017 as the theory whose EXTENSION to LLM information output is the open question, with c018 as the actual LLM-specific empirical step.

## Structural content worth knowing
- The contribution is a probabilistic-framework proof, not a measurement: convergence on one (even-more-accurate) algorithm reduces collective decision quality via correlated outcomes. It is an analytic result about decision systems.

## Caveats / limitations
- DOMAIN MISMATCH is the headline caveat (gate flagged it): decision-systems (hiring/lending screening), not LLM information output. The transfer to "many users get the same framing from one dominant model" is an analogy, not a result of this paper. The Drafter must label the transfer as untested when invoking c017.
