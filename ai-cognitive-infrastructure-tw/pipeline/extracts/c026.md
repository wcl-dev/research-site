# c026: Scaling language model size yields diminishing returns for single-message political persuasion

**URL**: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2413443122 (PNAS 2025; 403 on direct fetch — abstract captured verbatim from open arXiv version 2406.14508)
**Source type**: peer_reviewed (PNAS) | **Quality**: qs=5

**Access status**: ok (PNAS page 403'd; verbatim abstract from the authors' open arXiv preprint arXiv:2406.14508, same study)

## Directly addresses
- L4b (BOUNDING): the load-bearing bound on the persuasion link. Establishes a log-scaling law — persuasiveness has sharply diminishing returns in model size — so the chain cannot be inflated by assuming "bigger/dominant model ⇒ proportionally more persuasion."

## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for L4b — the log-scaling-law bound (verbatim abstract)
> Large language models can now generate political messages as persuasive as those written by humans, raising concerns about how far this persuasiveness may continue to increase with model size. Here, we generate 720 persuasive messages on 10 U.S. political issues from 24 language models spanning several orders of magnitude in size. We then deploy these messages in a large-scale randomized survey experiment (N = 25,982) to estimate the persuasive capability of each model. Our findings are twofold. First, we find evidence of a log scaling law: model persuasiveness is characterized by sharply diminishing returns, such that current frontier models are barely more persuasive than models smaller in size by an order of magnitude or more. Second, mere task completion (coherence, staying on topic) appears to account for larger models' persuasive advantage. These findings suggest that further scaling model size will not much increase the persuasiveness of static LLM-generated messages.

**Page/section**: Abstract (arXiv:2406.14508, verbatim)
**Why it matters**: This is the chain's containment wall. (a) "current frontier models are barely more persuasive than models smaller in size by an order of magnitude or more" — directly refutes any premise that a single dominant frontier model would have outsized persuasive reach versus the field. (b) "mere task completion ... appears to account for larger models' persuasive advantage" — what little advantage exists is from basic coherence, not from any special framing power. (c) "further scaling model size will not much increase the persuasiveness of static LLM-generated messages" — caps the L4b → L4c inflation at its source. Drafter should quote the "barely more persuasive" clause verbatim; it is the single most useful bounding sentence in the whole L4b cluster.

## Structural content worth knowing
- Design: 720 messages × 24 models across several orders of magnitude of size; N=25,982 randomized survey experiment. This is a much larger model-size sweep than the single-model RCTs (c023/c024), which is why it can estimate the SCALING relationship rather than a single point effect.
- The finding is a NULL on the scaling worry: the answer to "does persuasion keep rising with capability?" is essentially no (diminishing/log returns).

## Caveats / limitations
- "single-message" / "static" persuasion — this bounds one-shot generated messages, NOT necessarily long multi-turn personalized dialogue (cf. c024 Salvi, where personalization in live debate drives the effect). Drafter should pair c026 (static single message plateaus) with c024 (personalized dialogue is where the effect concentrates) to give the accurate, bounded picture rather than overstating either.
- US-only, 10 issues. Bound is demonstrated, not universal.
