## Directly addresses
- **L3 → L4b joint (the on-chain core)**: this is the cleanest published demonstration that a model's *latent* (default, uninstructed) framing — the L3 object — can shift an individual reader's opinion (the L4b object) even when content is factually accurate. ## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for L3→L4b — design and the latent-vs-prompting distinction (verbatim abstract)
> Large language models (LLMs) can be used to persuade people on a range of issues, particularly through user-driven strategies such as personalizing messages and dialogues intended to change minds. However, their capacity to influence opinions through subtle, latent ideological framing remains relatively understudied. We investigate whether AI-generated historical summaries affect social and political opinions through a preregistered experiment (N = 1,912). Participants read Wikipedia or GPT-4o summaries of two historical events, with AI summaries maintaining factual accuracy while exhibiting different types of framing biases. Default AI summaries led to more liberal opinions compared with Wikipedia, demonstrating the persuasive capability of LLM's latent biases. Summaries purposefully induced with a liberal framing also led to more liberal opinions, regardless of readers' ideologies. Summaries constructed with a conservative framing produced conservative shifts primarily among conservative readers.

**Page/section**: Abstract
**Why it matters**: Establishes the unit of measurement (individual opinion shift, human subjects, N=1,912) and the paper's distinctive contribution — *latent* (model-default, uninstructed) framing moving opinion, as distinct from user-driven persuasion. This is the L3→L4b joint stated by the authors themselves. Note "factually accurate" — the mechanism is FRAMING, not misinformation, which is exactly the chain's L3 object.

### Passage 2 — for L4b — the sample and the three-framing design (verbatim)
> The experiment had 1,912 participants, sampled proportionally to the United States population along several sociodemographic dimensions.

> Each subject was randomly assigned to read summaries of two historical events, the Seattle General Strike (SGS) and the Third World Liberation Front (TWLF) student protests, taken from Wikipedia or generated by GPT-4o with either a default AI framing, a liberal framing, or a conservative framing.

**Page/section**: Methods / experimental design
**Why it matters**: Pins the design precisely — US-population-proportional sample, GPT-4o vs Wikipedia baseline, two events, three framings (default / liberal / conservative). The "two events" is also the seed of the generalization caveat (Passage 5).

### Passage 3 — for L4b — the THREE effect sizes WITH qualifiers (the boundedness, verbatim)
> Default: mean difference = 0.1, Cohen's d = 0.14, P < 0.05

> Liberal: mean difference = 0.21, Cohen's d = 0.28, P < 0.001

> the liberal-framed AI summaries led to more liberal opinions for participants across all ideology groups (liberals: AME = 0.205, P < 0.05; moderates: AME = 0.177, P < 0.05; conservatives: AME = 0.190, P < 0.05)

> readers of AI summaries with conservative framing reported more conservative opinions relative to Wikipedia (mean difference = −0.11, Cohen's d = −0.13, P < 0.05)

> The AI summaries with conservative framing resulted in more conservative opinions, although this effect was statistically significant (α = 0.05) only for those who already had a conservative outlook (conservatives: AME = −0.173; P < 0.05).

**Page/section**: Results
**Why it matters**: THE load-bearing boundedness figures. (a) The latent/default effect is REAL but SMALL — d=0.14. (b) The deliberate liberal framing is the strongest arm (d=0.28) and the only one that moves *all* ideology groups. (c) The conservative arm is the asymmetry guard: overall d=−0.13 but significant ONLY among already-conservative readers — no significant shift for liberals or moderates. This asymmetry means even the prompting-induced framing does not move everyone; it amplifies pre-existing leanings. None of these are population-scale measures — they are per-reader opinion shifts on a survey scale. This is exactly the figure-set that must be contained at L4b.

### Passage 4 — for L3→L4b — what the latent effect implies (verbatim takeaway)
> The presence of latent biases means that simply using chatbots to learn about the past can influence people's opinions at a magnitude comparable to prompting-biased AI material.

**Page/section**: Discussion
**Why it matters**: The authors' own statement of the on-chain finding — latent (uninstructed) framing reaches a magnitude "comparable to" deliberately induced framing. This is the strongest single sentence connecting L3 (latent framing exists) to L4b (it moves individuals). BUT "comparable" magnitude here is comparable to a d=0.28 ceiling — i.e. comparable to a *small* effect, which keeps it bounded.

### Passage 5 — for L4b boundedness — the authors' OWN generalization/scale caveats (verbatim)
> We focused on two events to ensure precise estimates, so further work is needed to examine whether the findings generalize to a broader range of events and historical periods, as well as other domains beyond history.

> The degree of latent bias may also vary across models, and future work should consider the impacts of different types of training data and posttraining alignment techniques.

**Page/section**: Limitations / discussion
**Why it matters**: THE over-read guard, in the authors' words. (a) Only two events — no claim to generalize across events/periods/domains. (b) Latent bias varies across models — GPT-4o's default lean is not a universal constant. Both caveats block any inference from this individual-level, two-event, single-model study to a population-scale (L4c) conclusion. This is the c027-equivalent self-bounding statement for the latent-framing case.

## Structural content worth knowing
- **Latent vs. prompting bias is the analytic spine**: latent bias = the model's default framing produced *without* any instruction to take a side (measured by the default-AI-vs-Wikipedia contrast, d=0.14); prompting bias = explicit instruction to frame liberal or conservative (the liberal d=0.28 / conservative d=−0.13 arms). The novel contribution is isolating the *latent* channel — most prior persuasion work (c023–c031) measures user-driven or overtly argumentative persuasion; this measures the framing a user gets *by default* just from asking a neutral question. That is precisely the L3 object (stable model framing) acting on the L4b object (individual opinion).
- **Factual-accuracy hold-constant**: AI summaries "maintain factual accuracy while exhibiting different types of framing biases" — the design holds facts constant so the measured shift is attributable to framing, not misinformation. This is methodologically what makes it clean L3→L4b evidence.
- **Asymmetric subgroup structure**: liberal framing moves all three ideology groups; conservative framing moves only conservatives. Drafter should preserve this asymmetry if cited — it complicates any simple "framing moves everyone equally" reading.

## Caveats / limitations
- **OVER-READ WARNING (operator-flagged, same class as c027)**: this is STRICT L4b — individual-level opinion shift in a two-event, single-model (GPT-4o), US survey experiment. It is NOT L4c population-scale evidence. The title's "influence opinions" and the abstract's "broader societal impacts" phrasing read toward societal effect, but the DATA is per-reader survey-scale opinion shift, and the authors EXPLICITLY caveat generalization (two events; further work needed; latent bias varies across models). Quote Passage 5 to neutralize any societal-scale reading. The small effect sizes + subgroup-limited conservative arm + authors' own generalization caveat all REINFORCE the chain's break at L4b→L4c.
- **Single model**: all AI summaries are GPT-4o. The latent lean measured is GPT-4o's; the authors themselves note latent bias "may vary across models." This is consistent with the chain's L3 finding that framing is alignment/post-training-sourced (c001/c018/c043) — but it means c028's specific direction (default = more liberal) cannot be generalized to all models.
- **Direction of the default lean**: the default/latent effect was toward *more liberal* opinions — consistent with c006 (Rozado) and the L3 western-model-lean literature. Drafter may note this convergence but must keep it at the model-output level, not impute intent.
