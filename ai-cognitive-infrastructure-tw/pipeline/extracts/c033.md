## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for L4c — simulation, not measurement (verbatim abstract)
> Accurately simulating human opinion dynamics is crucial for understanding a variety of societal phenomena, including polarization and the spread of misinformation. However, the agent-based models (ABMs) commonly used for such simulations often over-simplify human behavior. We propose a new approach to simulating opinion dynamics based on populations of Large Language Models (LLMs). Our findings reveal a strong inherent bias in LLM agents towards producing accurate information, leading simulated agents to consensus in line with scientific reality. This bias limits their utility for understanding resistance to consensus views on issues like climate change. After inducing confirmation bias through prompt engineering, however, we observed opinion fragmentation in line with existing agent-based modeling and opinion dynamics research. These insights highlight the promise and limitations of LLM agents in this domain and suggest a path forward: refining LLMs with real-world discourse to better simulate the evolution of human beliefs.

**Page/section**: Abstract (verbatim)
**Why it matters**: (a) "simulating opinion dynamics based on populations of Large Language Models (LLMs)" — the agents ARE LLMs; there are no human subjects. This is not L4c evidence about humans. (b) The simulation's own finding cuts AGAINST naive transfer: LLM agents have "a strong inherent bias ... towards producing accurate information, leading simulated agents to consensus" — they had to "induc[e] confirmation bias through prompt engineering" to get human-like fragmentation. So even the simulation does not behave like a human population without manual manipulation. (c) The closing line ("refining LLMs with real-world discourse to better simulate the evolution of human beliefs") concedes the gap — current LLM-agent simulation is not yet a faithful model of human belief evolution.

## Structural content worth knowing
- Networks of LLM agents in an ABM-style opinion-dynamics setup; the contribution is methodological (LLMs as ABM agents), with the honest caveat that the agents are too consensus-prone to model real polarization without prompt-engineered bias.

## Caveats / limitations
- HARD LABEL: simulation only, qs=3. The Drafter must never present this as evidence that LLMs shift real population opinion. Together with c032 (framework) and c034 (analogy-only), it documents that the L4c empirical cell has zero direct LLM-on-human-population studies.
