# c036: Teens, Social Media and AI Chatbots 2025 (Pew Research Center)

## Directly addresses
- L4a / L1 (usage distribution): the authoritative population-representative datapoint on chatbot adoption among US teens, simultaneously showing ChatGPT concentration AND multi-chatbot spread — nuancing both the L4a dominance premise and the multi-model counter.

## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for L4a — overall adoption + concentration-with-diversity (verbatim)
> Roughly two-thirds of teens (64%) say they ever use an AI chatbot.

> ChatGPT (59%) is by far the most widely used chatbot and the only one we measured that a majority of teens use. This is more than twice the rate of the next most commonly used chatbots: Gemini (23%) and Meta AI (20%). Fewer say they use Copilot, Character.ai and Claude.

**Page/section**: AI chatbots usage section
**Why it matters**: Two findings the Drafter needs in tension: (a) CONCENTRATION — ChatGPT at 59% is "by far the most widely used" and "the only one ... a majority of teens use," more than twice the next (this is the seed of the single-model-dominance worry L4a depends on); (b) DIVERSITY — usage still spreads across Gemini (23%), Meta AI (20%), Copilot, Character.ai, Claude. Population-representative data showing a dominant model AND a diverse field at once. This is the most authoritative usage record in the set and lets the Drafter characterize L4a's premise honestly: dominance is real but not exclusive.

## Structural content worth knowing
- 64% of teens ever use a chatbot; ChatGPT 59% / Gemini 23% / Meta AI 20% / then Copilot, Character.ai, Claude (lower). Pew population-representative, US teens 13-17.

## Caveats / limitations
- CONTAINMENT (gate-flagged): this is adoption-distribution data, NOT cognition. It must not migrate into L4b/L4c. Drafter uses it to characterize the dominance/diversity question at L4a/L1 only.
- US teens specifically — population-scoped; useful precisely because it is representative, but not generalizable to all users.
