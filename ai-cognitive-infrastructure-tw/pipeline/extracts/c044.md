# c044: Performance and biases of Large Language Models in public opinion simulation (Qu & Wang)

## Directly addresses
- CE / §6 (the L4c-inference guard): the methodological caution against treating LLM output as a measure of real population opinion — exactly the inference the L4c leap requires. This is the strongest anti-overclaim anchor for the conditional-policy frame.

## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for CE/§6 — representational gaps + the use-with-conventional-methods caution (verbatim abstract)
> The rise of Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT marks a pivotal advancement in artificial intelligence, reshaping the landscape of data analysis and processing. By simulating public opinion, ChatGPT shows promise in facilitating public policy development. However, challenges persist regarding its worldwide applicability and bias across demographics and themes. Our research employs socio-demographic data from the World Values Survey to evaluate ChatGPT's performance in diverse contexts. Findings indicate significant performance disparities, especially when comparing countries. Models perform better in Western, English-speaking, and developed nations, notably the United States, in comparison to others. Disparities also manifest across demographic groups, showing biases related to gender, ethnicity, age, education, and social class. The study further uncovers thematic biases in political and environmental simulations. These results highlight the need to enhance LLMs' representativeness and address biases, ensuring their equitable and effective integration into public opinion research alongside conventional methodologies.

**Page/section**: Abstract (verbatim)
**Why it matters**: This is the §6 caution made empirical. (a) "significant performance disparities, especially when comparing countries ... Models perform better in Western, English-speaking, and developed nations" plus demographic biases (gender, ethnicity, age, education, social class) — LLM output is a SYSTEMATICALLY GAPPED proxy for population opinion, not a faithful mirror. (b) The closing prescription — "integration into public opinion research alongside conventional methodologies" — explicitly positions LLM output as a SUPPLEMENT, not a substitute for measuring real populations. The Drafter uses this to argue the L4c leap (inferring societal cognition from model output) is methodologically unwarranted: even using LLMs to SIMULATE opinion is biased, let alone inferring real population shifts from a dominant model's framing.

## Structural content worth knowing
- WVS-benchmarked evaluation across countries and demographic groups; documents both geographic (Western/English skew) and demographic (gender/ethnicity/age/education/class) representational gaps, plus thematic biases (political, environmental).

## Caveats / limitations
- This is about LLMs simulating opinion (a methodological use), which is the inverse direction of the chain (LLM output shaping opinion). But the load-bearing transfer is the same caution: LLM output ≠ population opinion. Drafter should frame it precisely as a methodological caution that bites the inference direction L4c needs.
