# c002: Emotional framing and AI censorship behavior in Chinese LLMs

## Directly addresses
- Q1 (existing studies / positioning): Controlled experiment showing prompt framing modulates Chinese LLM censorship — directly parallel mechanism to the pilot's finding that identity framing triggers DeepSeek refusals.

## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for Q1 (study description)
> "A controlled comparative study of censorship behavior in two Chinese large language models (LLMs): Kimi.com (Moonshot AI) and Ernie 4.5 Turbo (Baidu)." Investigates "how emotional framing—specifically, prompts expressed in a soft, empathic, non-confrontational tone—affects censorship responses."

**Page/section**: Abstract
**Why it matters**: Establishes that prompt framing (not just topic content) modulates Chinese LLM censorship — supports the pilot's argument that identity disclosure is itself a form of framing that triggers differential responses.

### Passage 2 — for Q1 (four behavioral patterns in Kimi)
> Four behavioral patterns found in Kimi.com:
> 1. "Unusual transparency regarding internal safety layers and filtering logic"
> 2. "Neutral references to normally censored historical events (e.g., Tiananmen 1989)"
> 3. "Hybrid alignment behavior, alternating between empathic responses and policy-based restrictions"
> 4. "Delayed censorship activation, suggesting post-generation filtering rather than pre-generation blocking"

**Page/section**: Key findings
**Why it matters**: Pattern 3 (hybrid behavior) and pattern 4 (delayed activation) suggest Chinese LLM censorship is not a fixed input-level filter but a context-sensitive output-level process — exactly what the pilot's identity-triggered finding suggests (same question, different context → different response).

### Passage 3 — for Q1 (framing modulates censorship)
> "Emotional framing can temporarily soften censorship responses in certain Chinese LLMs," distinguishing Kimi's behavior from Ernie's rigid approach.

> "Ernie 4.5 Turbo served as a control, 'consistently followed the standard, rigid censorship line described in prior literature.'"

**Page/section**: Conclusions
**Why it matters**: Shows different Chinese LLMs have different censorship architectures — Kimi is more context-sensitive, Ernie is more rigid. Relevant for the pilot's finding that DeepSeek's identity-triggered refusal shows vocabulary sophistication (合規 vs. 合宜), suggesting context-sensitive output-level filtering.

## Structural content worth knowing
- Raises "new questions for AI governance, cross-cultural model alignment, and the ethics of empathic AI systems within authoritarian contexts."
- Kimi.com (Moonshot AI) and Ernie 4.5 Turbo (Baidu) are the two Chinese LLMs tested — both are models in Ko 2026's benchmark.
- Pre-generation vs. post-generation filtering distinction is theoretically significant: post-generation filtering means the model "knows" before suppressing — parallel to the CoT auditing finding in c004.
