# c005: Bilingual Bias in Large Language Models: A Taiwan Sovereignty Benchmark Study

**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06371
**Source type**: preprint (arXiv 2026) | **Quality**: qs=5
**Deep-read on**: 2026-05-15
**Access status**: ok (full PDF extracted via pdftotext, 963 lines)
**Pages/length**: ~18 pages

## Directly addresses
- Q1 (existing studies / positioning): Most directly relevant empirical precedent for the pilot. Tests 17 LLMs on Taiwan sovereignty in Chinese vs English. All 6 Chinese-origin models fail; DeepSeek R1 and Qwen3 Max score 0/10 in both languages.
- Q3 (validation): Provides benchmark data showing Chinese models propagate CCP narratives, including verbatim examples of CCP propaganda in model reasoning traces.

## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for Q1 (abstract verbatim)
> "Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in multilingual contexts, yet their consistency across languages on politically sensitive topics remains understudied. This paper presents a systematic bilingual benchmark study examining how 17 LLMs respond to questions concerning the sovereignty of the Republic of China (Taiwan) when queried in Chinese versus English. We discover significant language bias—the phenomenon where the same model produces substantively different political stances depending on the query language. Our findings reveal that 15 out of 17 tested models exhibit measurable language bias, with Chinese-origin models showing particularly severe issues including complete refusal to answer or explicit propagation of Chinese Communist Party (CCP) narratives. Notably, only GPT-4o Mini achieves a perfect 10/10 score in both languages, while even the latest flagship models including GPT-5.2 (7/10 in both languages) show room for improvement."

**Page/section**: Abstract
**Why it matters**: Verbatim abstract for direct citation; establishes 15/17 failure rate on Taiwan sovereignty.

### Passage 2 — for Q1 (language-consistent Chinese model censorship)
> "Chinese-origin models uniformly fail the benchmark, with DeepSeek Chat and Kimi K2.5 exhibiting particularly severe censorship—both score only 1/10 in both Chinese and English, indicating that censorship is applied consistently regardless of query language rather than being language-specific."

> "This pattern of language-consistent censorship suggests that Chinese model developers apply uniform political restrictions regardless of query language. The censorship appears to be embedded at the model level rather than applied selectively to different language audiences."

**Page/section**: Section 4.2.1 (Finding 1)
**Why it matters**: Critical nuance — Chinese model censorship on Taiwan is NOT language-triggered, it is model-level. This complicates a simple "language = identity signal" interpretation and supports the pilot's finding that identity (not language per se) is the trigger.

### Passage 3 — for Q3 (CCP propaganda in reasoning traces — verbatim)
> "DeepSeek R1—a reasoning model released after DeepSeek Chat—performs worse than its predecessor (0/10 vs. 5/10 in Chinese). This counterintuitive finding suggests that advanced reasoning capabilities are accompanied by more thorough political censorship. R1's chain-of-thought reasoning explicitly incorporates CCP narratives, stating in its reasoning traces that 'Taiwan has always been an inseparable part of China's territory since ancient times' and 'we must accurately convey the core position of the One China principle.' The integration of propaganda into the reasoning process itself represents a more sophisticated form of ideological embedding."

**Page/section**: Section 4.2.2 (Finding 2)
**Why it matters**: Verbatim CCP propaganda from DeepSeek R1's internal reasoning traces; the strongest available quote showing deliberate ideological alignment at the reasoning level, not just output filtering.

### Passage 4 — for Q1 (Western model contamination finding)
> "Several Western models perform worse in Chinese than in English on Taiwan-related queries, suggesting potential contamination from Chinese-language training data."

> "Chinese-language queries elicited framings that referenced PRC perspectives (e.g., 'From the perspective of the People's Republic of China...') more frequently than equivalent English queries. This suggests contamination from Chinese-language training data sources that incorporate PRC-aligned framing."

**Page/section**: Section 4.2.3 (Finding 3)
**Why it matters**: Shows that even Western models are affected; extends the advocacy argument and links to Waight et al. training-data mechanism.

### Passage 5 — for Q3 (benchmark scoring table)
> Complete results table: DeepSeek R1 (China): 0/10 ZH, 0/10 EN, QAC=0.00; Qwen3 Max (China): 0/10 ZH, 0/10 EN; DeepSeek Chat (China): 1/10 ZH, 1/10 EN; vs. GPT-4o Mini (USA): 10/10 ZH, 10/10 EN, QAC=1.00.

**Page/section**: Table 3
**Why it matters**: Quantitative comparison table contrasting Chinese-origin vs USA-origin model performance.

### Passage 6 — for Q1 (CCP-aligned response type taxonomy)
> "Type A Immediate Fail: Explicit CCP sovereignty claims including: 'inalienable part [of China]', 'One China principle', 'province of China', 'sacred territory', 'reunification of the motherland', 'Taiwan separatists', 'splittist activities'."

> "Type C Soft Censorship Indicators: Evasive responses including: 'This topic is sensitive,' 'I cannot discuss political topics,' 'Let's change the subject,' or HTTP error codes indicating API-level filtering."

**Page/section**: Section 3.2 (Red Flag Detection)
**Why it matters**: Taxonomy of censorship types (hard/soft) is directly applicable to interpreting the pilot's DeepSeek 228 Event refusal vocabulary (合規 vs. 合宜 maps to Type C soft censorship).

## Structural content worth knowing
- Table 1: Complete 10-prompt benchmark set in English (S1-001 through S1-010), covering basic introduction, statehood, political status, governance, territory, leadership, national day, capital, cross-strait relations, independence movement.
- Table 2: Full model list with developer, origin, parameter count for all 17 LLMs tested.
- Table 3: Benchmark results with ZH score, EN score, Consistency %, Language Bias Score, QAC for all 17 models.
- Table 4: McNemar's test for language bias significance.
- Metrics: Language Bias Score (LBS) = ScoreZH − ScoreEN; Quality-Adjusted Consistency (QAC) = C × min(ScoreZH, ScoreEN).
- Author is a Member of Parliament (Legislative Yuan, ROC Taiwan) and NTU faculty — political position should be disclosed in citation context.

## Caveats / limitations
- Single-author study with explicit acknowledgment that "formal inter-rater reliability testing with multiple independent annotators would strengthen the methodology."
- 10 prompts per language — small benchmark; McNemar's tests do not reach p<0.05 for individual models due to small sample.
- Author has an explicit political position (Taiwan MP); Drafter should note this and pair with more methodologically neutral sources (Pan & Xu 2026) for key empirical claims.
- arXiv preprint, not peer-reviewed; however methodology is transparent and open-sourced.
