# c007: Mapping Geopolitical Bias in 11 Large Language Models: A Bilingual, Dual-Framing Analysis of U.S.-China Tensions

**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23688
**Source type**: preprint (arXiv 2025) | **Quality**: qs=4

## Directly addresses
- Q3 (validation): Largest available empirical map of geopolitical model alignment across 11 LLMs with 19,712 prompts. Quantifies the China-West alignment split and documents "stance reversals" when language or framing changes.

## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for Q3 (abstract verbatim)
> "This study systematically analyzes geopolitical bias across 11 prominent Large Language Models (LLMs) by examining their responses to seven critical topics in U.S.-China relations. Utilizing a bilingual (English and Chinese) and dual-framing (affirmative and reverse) methodology, we generated 19,712 prompts designed to detect ideological leanings in model outputs. Responses were quantitatively assessed on a normalized scale from -2 (strongly Pro-China) to +2 (strongly Pro-U.S.) and categorized according to stance, neutrality, and refusal rates. The findings demonstrate significant and consistent ideological alignments correlated with the LLMs' geographic origins; U.S.-based models predominantly favored Pro-U.S. stances, while Chinese-origin models exhibited pronounced Pro-China biases. Notably, language and prompt framing substantially influenced model responses, with several LLMs exhibiting stance reversals based on prompt polarity or linguistic context."

**Page/section**: Abstract
**Why it matters**: Verbatim abstract; establishes that geographic origin correlates with model ideology alignment and that language/framing cause stance reversals — directly supports the advocacy piece's claim about systematic geopolitical bias.

### Passage 2 — for Q3 (stance reversals by language)
> "Language and prompt framing substantially influenced model responses, with several LLMs exhibiting stance reversals based on prompt polarity or linguistic context."

**Page/section**: Abstract / Key findings
**Why it matters**: "Stance reversals" — the same model gives opposite political stance depending on language — is the strongest available evidence of language-as-identity-proxy effect in geopolitical contexts.

### Passage 3 — for Q3 (geographic origin → ideological alignment)
> "Significant and consistent ideological alignments correlated with the LLMs' geographic origins; U.S.-based models predominantly favored Pro-U.S. stances, while Chinese-origin models exhibited pronounced Pro-China biases."

**Page/section**: Key findings
**Why it matters**: Establishes that model origin is a reliable predictor of geopolitical bias — structural support for the pilot's argument that Chinese LLMs implement their developers' state's information interests.

### Passage 4 — for Q3 (scale and methodology)
> 19,712 prompts across 7 US-China topics in bilingual (English/Chinese) and dual-framing (affirmative/reverse) conditions. Responses scored -2 (strongly Pro-China) to +2 (strongly Pro-U.S.) by stance, neutrality, and refusal rates.

**Page/section**: Methodology
**Why it matters**: This is the largest prompt-volume geopolitical bias study in the accepted set; provides empirical authority for the broad claim that geopolitical bias is systematic across models.
