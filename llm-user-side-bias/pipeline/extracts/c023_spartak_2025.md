# c023: China's Information Sovereignty Under Transnational Communication Processes

## Directly addresses
- Q2 (geopolitical frameworks): Comprehensive analysis of China's information sovereignty as deliberate state strategy. Essential for arguing Chinese LLM censorship is state-level information policy, not corporate error.

## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for Q2 (China's information sovereignty definition)
> "China defines information sovereignty through cyber sovereignty, described as 'the right of the state to control the Internet within its borders and independently decide questions of data flows and cybersecurity.'"

**Page/section**: Definition section
**Why it matters**: Verbatim definition of China's sovereignty concept; provides citable definition distinct from Western frameworks.

### Passage 2 — for Q2 (three-pillar strategy)
> China integrates "legislative, technological, and cultural measures to maintain control over its information space while strengthening its position in global digital competition."

> Three pillars: (1) Legislative: "Three major laws enacted 2016-2021 (Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law)"; (2) Technological: "Advanced monitoring systems and content filtration"; (3) Cultural: "Promotion of traditional values as defense against Western influence."

**Page/section**: Strategy analysis
**Why it matters**: Shows Chinese LLM content control is embedded in a multi-layered state architecture — not an isolated corporate decision but part of a legislated, institutionalized system.

### Passage 3 — for Q2 (AI in China's information control — quantified)
> "Automated systems 'allow real-time analysis of enormous volumes of information entering the Internet, and block or delete content considered undesirable or threatening state security.'"

> "The systems employ 'advanced text, image and video recognition technologies capable of identifying not only explicit violations, but hidden hints or metaphors related to politically sensitive topics.'"

> "In 2017, over 100,000 websites and 30 million posts were blocked; by 2023, 'over 1,000 websites and over 50 mobile applications' containing 'inaccurate information' faced removal."

**Page/section**: AI in information control
**Why it matters**: Scale and technical sophistication of Chinese information control infrastructure; provides historical data points showing AI is central to this system.

### Passage 4 — for Q2 (deliberate state coordination — institutional)
> "'The Central Leading Group on Cybersecurity and the Communist Party of China coordinate implementation of state strategy'"

> Xi Jinping "'established the Central Committee on Cybersecurity and Informatization, coordinating policy, economic, cultural, society, defense and other digital security spheres'"

**Page/section**: Institutional structure
**Why it matters**: Shows high-level CCP coordination of information sovereignty — connects to framing Chinese LLM censorship as an expression of top-down party-state information policy.

### Passage 5 — for Q2 (contrast with Western model)
> China's model as "fundamentally state-centric" emphasizing: "Sovereignty principle: 'The right of the state to control the Internet within its borders'"; "Data localization: Strict requirements for domestic data storage"; "State platform development: Supporting domestic companies (Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent) rather than permitting foreign dominance"; "Cultural preservation: Active resistance to 'Western ideologies, such as individualism and liberalism'"

**Page/section**: China vs. West comparison
**Why it matters**: Explicitly frames China's approach as a sovereign state-centric alternative to the liberal/democratic approach — supports the advocacy piece's framing of Chinese LLM censorship as a different political model, not just corporate policy.

## Structural content worth knowing
- Paper analyzes three legislative pillars: Cybersecurity Law (2017), Data Security Law (2021), Personal Information Protection Law (2021) — together constituting the legal architecture within which DeepSeek and other Chinese LLMs operate.
- Discusses "platform sovereignty" — supporting domestic tech companies as a vector for information control.
- Xi Jinping's personal role in establishing cybersecurity coordination structures documented.
- Author: Sergei Spartak — Russian-language researcher; perspective may reflect particular framing of Chinese-Russian alignment on information sovereignty vs. Western model; Drafter should note this possible framing effect.

## Caveats / limitations
- Author is Russian (Sergei Spartak); potential framing alignment with non-Western information sovereignty models — use for factual structural analysis of China's approach, not normative evaluation.
- Published in socionauki.ru (Russian journal); less familiar to Western/Taiwan-facing audiences; pair with Atlantic Council sources for credibility in English/Chinese advocacy contexts.
