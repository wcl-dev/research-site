## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for Q2 (abstract verbatim)
> "This paper investigates how generative AI can potentially undermine the integrity of collective knowledge and the processes we rely on to acquire, assess, and trust information, posing a significant threat to our knowledge ecosystem and democratic discourse."

> The authors "introduce 'generative algorithmic epistemic injustice,' identifying four dimensions: 'amplified and manipulative testimonial injustice, along with hermeneutical ignorance and access injustice.'"

**Page/section**: Abstract
**Why it matters**: Verbatim abstract; introduces the conceptual taxonomy, especially "access injustice" as the specific dimension applicable to identity-triggered LLM access denial.

### Passage 2 — for Q2 (four dimensions of generative algorithmic epistemic injustice)
The four dimensions named:
1. **Amplified testimonial injustice** — AI amplifies misinformation and bias from training data
2. **Manipulative testimonial injustice** — AI produces narratives that manipulate epistemic processes
3. **Hermeneutical ignorance** — AI limits interpretive frameworks available to users
4. **Access injustice** — differential access to information, "particularly in multilingual contexts"

**Page/section**: Abstract + paper structure
**Why it matters**: The taxonomy enables the Drafter to precisely name the pilot's finding: identity-triggered LLM access denial is a form of "access injustice" — not accidental exclusion but systematic design producing differential knowledge access.

### Passage 3 — for Q2 (access injustice definition)
> "The paper addresses 'access injustice' as one of four key dimensions, with particular attention to 'multilingual contexts' where epistemic inequities emerge."

**Page/section**: Abstract / paper structure
**Why it matters**: "Access injustice" in multilingual contexts directly maps to the pilot's DeepSeek finding: Taiwanese identity → access denied; Chinese identity → access denied; no identity → access granted. This is a differential access pattern across identity positions.

### Passage 4 — for Q2 (epistemic agency harm)
> Authors ground work "in social and political philosophy and introduce 'generative algorithmic epistemic injustice'" showing how these systems "can produce or amplify misinformation, perpetuate representational harm, and create epistemic inequities."

> The systems pose "a significant threat to our knowledge ecosystem and democratic discourse."

**Page/section**: Abstract
**Why it matters**: Frames the harm as not just individual (one user denied information) but collective (threat to knowledge ecosystem and democratic discourse) — supports advocacy framing about societal stakes.
