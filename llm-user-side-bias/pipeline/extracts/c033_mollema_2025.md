## Directly addresses
- Q2 (theoretical frameworks / algorithmic discrimination): Provides taxonomy for classifying LLM epistemic injustice types including "generative hermeneutical erasure." Enables precise categorization of identity-triggered LLM access denial as a specific and nameable injustice type.

## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for Q2 (taxonomy of AI epistemic injustice — abstract verbatim)
> "Epistemic injustice related to AI is a growing concern. In relation to machine learning models, epistemic injustice can have a diverse range of sources, ranging from epistemic opacity, the discriminatory automation of testimonial prejudice, and the distortion of human beliefs via generative AI's hallucinations to the exclusion of the global South in global AI governance, the execution of bureaucratic violence via algorithmic systems, and interactions with conversational artificial agents."

**Page/section**: Abstract
**Why it matters**: Maps the landscape of AI epistemic injustice types; the "discriminatory automation of testimonial prejudice" category directly covers identity-triggered differential access.

### Passage 2 — for Q2 (generative hermeneutical erasure — definition)
> "Generative hermeneutical erasure" is described as "the automation of 'epistemicide', the injustice done to epistemic agents in their capacity for collective sense-making through the suppression of difference in epistemology and conceptualization by LLMs."

> The mechanism: "AI systems' 'view from nowhere' epistemically inferiorizes non-Western epistemologies and thereby contributes to the erosion of their epistemic particulars, gradually contributing to hermeneutical erasure."

**Page/section**: Hermeneutical erasure section
**Why it matters**: Provides the specific concept of "epistemicide" — the destruction of a knowledge framework through suppression. Applied to the pilot: when a Taiwanese user is denied access to information about the 228 Event, their epistemic framework (Taiwanese historical understanding) is being systematically suppressed.

### Passage 3 — for Q2 (sources taxonomy)
> AI epistemic injustice sources identified: (1) Epistemic opacity, (2) Discriminatory automation of testimonial prejudice, (3) Distortion of human beliefs via hallucinations, (4) Exclusion of Global South from AI governance, (5) Bureaucratic violence via algorithmic systems, (6) Interactions with conversational agents.

**Page/section**: Taxonomy section
**Why it matters**: The taxonomy enables the pilot study to precisely locate its contribution — items 2 (discriminatory automation of prejudice) and 6 (conversational agent interactions) cover the identity-triggered access denial finding.

## Structural content worth knowing
- Paper is 33 pages; peer-reviewed in AI Ethics journal (Springer, 2025).
- "View from nowhere" epistemology critique: LLMs claim neutrality while embedding particular (typically Western/hegemonic) epistemological frameworks.
- "Epistemicide" concept borrowed from decolonial scholarship — powerful framing for the advocacy piece but may require careful contextualization for a Taiwan audience.
- The paper is accessible via arXiv:2504.07531.

## Caveats / limitations
- Theoretical/philosophical paper; no empirical data.
- "Hermeneutical erasure" concept focuses on suppression of non-Western epistemologies broadly — the pilot's case is more specific (national identity triggering access denial on a particular historical event).
- Drafter should use for conceptual vocabulary, not empirical citation.
