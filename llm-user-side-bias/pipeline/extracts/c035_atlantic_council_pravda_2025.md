## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for Q2/Q3 (Pravda network definition)
> "A collection of fraudulent news portals targeting more than eighty countries and regions" that was "launched by Russia in 2014." The operation functions as "an information laundromat, amplifying and saturating the news cycle" with content from Russian state outlets and Kremlin channels.

**Page/section**: Introduction
**Why it matters**: Establishes the Pravda network as a documented case of state-backed information poisoning infrastructure — provides the Russian example the advocacy piece can use in parallel with China's LLM censorship.

### Passage 2 — for Q2/Q3 (AI poisoning mechanism)
> The network manipulates AI through two vectors: (1) "Wikipedia infiltration: By 'posing as authoritative sources on Wikipedia,' the network embeds pro-Kremlin narratives into content that trains language models." (2) "LLM training contamination: The fraudulent portals present themselves as 'reliable news outlets cited by popular large language models,' thereby corrupting the datasets upon which AI systems rely."

**Page/section**: How it poisons AI
**Why it matters**: Explains the training-data poisoning mechanism — distinct from China's content-filter approach but illustrative of the same principle: geopolitical actors using AI infrastructure as a control vector.

### Passage 3 — for Q3 (evidence of effectiveness)
> "Russian tropes are rewriting the story of Russia's war in Ukraine," with direct consequences: Western audiences encounter "pro-Kremlin, anti-Ukrainian, and anti-Western messaging when using AI chatbots."

> The operation deliberately targets "NATO, as well as Trump, French President Emmanuel Macron, and other world leaders" during critical electoral periods.

**Page/section**: Evidence section
**Why it matters**: Demonstrates that AI training poisoning produces measurable effects on chatbot output — evidence that geopolitical actors can succeed in using AI as information control infrastructure.

### Passage 4 — for Q2/Q3 (scope and scale)
> The network operates across "more than eighty countries and regions," with coordinated activity spiking during "Romania and Moldova" elections, US-Ukrainian discussions, and the 2024 "super-election year."

**Page/section**: Scale section
**Why it matters**: 80+ countries = global reach of a single state-actor AI poisoning operation — supports the advocacy claim that LLM geopolitical bias is not a local issue but a global information sovereignty problem.

### Passage 5 — for Q3 (implications)
> "As AI chatbots continue to advance, Russia is infecting them with Kremlin-manipulated content," raising questions about "the transparency of the training of AI models" and whether content "from known Russian-manipulated sources" receives adequate moderation.

**Page/section**: Implications
**Why it matters**: Frames the accountability problem — lack of training data transparency enables geopolitical information control. Applicable to the advocacy piece's argument about the need for AI transparency regardless of origin.
