# c041: Chinese Discourse Power: Capabilities and Impact

**URL**: https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/in-depth-research-reports/report/chinese-discourse-power-capabilities-and-impact/
**Source type**: think_tank (Atlantic Council / DFRLab) | **Quality**: qs=4

**Access status**: ok (full text accessible via Atlantic Council)
**Pages/length**: ~80 pages (major research report)

## Directly addresses
- Q2 (geopolitical frameworks): Documents Chinese state discourse power projection as a deliberate strategy — institutional backing for framing Chinese LLM censorship as part of a state information control apparatus.

## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for Q2 (discourse power definition)
> "Narrative agenda-setting ability focused on reshaping global governance, values, and norms to legitimize and facilitate the expression of state power."

**Page/section**: Definition section
**Why it matters**: Defines "discourse power" — this is the macro-level geopolitical concept within which LLM censorship operates as a micro-level implementation.

### Passage 2 — for Q2 (scale of China's information infrastructure)
> "Over $1.5 billion spent annually on propaganda since 2008."

> "Xinhua employs the world's largest foreign correspondent network."

> "Over 200 million overseas social media followers across various platforms" as of 2021.

> Xinhua distributes "seven thousand three hundred articles, photos, videos, and other media content in fifteen languages daily."

**Page/section**: Capabilities section
**Why it matters**: Quantifies China's information projection capacity — context for understanding Chinese LLMs as an extension of this infrastructure.

### Passage 3 — for Q2 (AI and big data in discourse projection)
> China launched "four State Key Laboratories dedicated to using big data to better tailor content to specific audiences."

> Chinese firms like Nebula use "big data and cloud computing technologies" to analyze international public opinion, enabling more "targeted information operations."

**Page/section**: AI and digital platforms section
**Why it matters**: Documents China's use of AI and big data for targeted information projection — contextualizes Chinese LLMs as part of an AI-enabled narrative control apparatus, not isolated commercial products.

### Passage 4 — for Q2 (discourse power + cyber sovereignty alignment)
> Three strategic vectors: Channel expansion across platforms; "Content innovation through 'precise communication' tailored to audiences"; "Governance of technological standards (promoting 'cyber sovereignty')."

**Page/section**: Strategy section
**Why it matters**: "Promoting cyber sovereignty" as a strategic vector links discourse power projection to the information sovereignty framework in c023 — provides a coherent narrative arc for the advocacy piece.

### Passage 5 — for Q2/Q3 (global reach emphasis)
> "China's efforts gain larger ground in countries where civic freedoms are already limited" and where "elite capture matters more than public opinion."

> The report concludes China is "creating an alternative order in the Global South."

**Page/section**: Global implications
**Why it matters**: Frames Chinese information projection as an alternative global order challenge — relevant for Taiwan-facing audience about why this matters beyond China's borders.

## Structural content worth knowing
- Report examines both external (overseas) and internal (domestic) discourse power capabilities.
- "Media convergence" framework: integration of internal/external propaganda across channels with centralized CCP oversight.
- Domestic companies (Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent, Huawei) are positioned as discourse power infrastructure components — directly applicable to DeepSeek's role in this architecture.
- Democratic counter-strategy: "clearly articulate advantages of democratic approaches to technological governance."

## Caveats / limitations
- 2023 think-tank report — pre-DeepSeek release; does not address LLMs specifically.
- Drafter should use for macro-level framing of China's information strategy; LLM-specific claims require connection to c001, c003, c004 empirical sources.
- Atlantic Council is a Western-aligned institution; perspective may overstate Chinese government coordination of commercial entities.
