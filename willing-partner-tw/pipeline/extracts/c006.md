## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for Q2 (similar-content detection, the brief's core "比對基準" claim)
> "We use our technology to detect content that is the same or almost exactly the same as that rated by fact checkers, and add notices to that content as well."

**Section**: "Clearly labeling misinformation and informing people about it"
**Why it matters**: This is the exact sentence the Gatekeeper flagged as needing raw verification before any quote enters the draft — **confirmed verbatim, identical in the 2025-03-28 and 2026-01-03 snapshots**. This is the brief's permitted phrasing for "rating 作為相似內容比對基準": Meta explicitly says it detects near-duplicate content and applies the same notice — this is the mechanism, worded by Meta itself, not "training data."

### Passage 2 — for Q2 (the scope limit on similarity matching — an important qualifier)
> "We generally do not add notices to content that makes a similar claim rated by fact checkers, if the content is not identical. This is because small differences in how a claim is phrased might change whether it is true or false."

**Section**: same list, immediately following Passage 1
**Why it matters**: Qualifies Passage 1 — the matching is near-duplicate detection, not claim-level semantic matching. Important for not over-claiming the mechanism's reach; Meta itself draws this line.

### Passage 3 — for Q2 (demotion mechanism, "reduce")
> "Once a fact-checker rates a piece of content as False, Altered, or Partly False, or we detect it as near identical, it may receive reduced distribution on Facebook, Instagram and Threads. We dramatically reduce the distribution of False and Altered posts, and reduce the distribution of Partly False to a lesser extent. For Missing Context, we focus on surfacing more information from fact checkers. Meta does not suggest content to users once it is rated by a fact-checker, which significantly reduces the number of people who see it."

**Section**: "Ensuring fewer people see misinformation"
**Why it matters**: This is Meta's own graduated-severity description of the "reduce" leg of the Remove/Reduce/Inform framework first documented in c002 (2019 TechNews) — confirms the same three-part framework is still operative in 2025-26, and gives the severity gradient (False/Altered > Partly False > Missing Context) verbatim.

### Passage 4 — for Q2/Q4 (current rest-of-world mechanism, post-Jan-2025 US changes)
> "Today in the rest of the world, we rely on fact-checkers who are independent from Meta and certified through the non-partisan International Fact-Checking Network (IFCN) or, in Europe, the European Fact-Checking Standards Network (EFCSN) to address misinformation on Facebook, Instagram, and Threads. While fact-checkers focus on the legitimacy and accuracy of information, we focus on taking action by informing people when content has been rated."

**Section**: preceding Passage 1, cross-linking to the Jan 2025 "More Speech and Fewer Mistakes" announcement and the Community Notes feature page
**Why it matters**: Confirms — in Meta's own current wording — that as of the snapshot dates the 3PFC/IFCN model is explicitly framed as a **"rest of the world"** arrangement, distinct from the US's Community Notes replacement. This directly supports the brief's framing that Taiwan (rest-of-world) sits in the "mechanism intact, funding uncertain" quadrant rather than "already replaced" as of the TFC contract-crisis reporting in c009.
