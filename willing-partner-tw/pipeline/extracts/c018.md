## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for Q5 (abstract, verbatim — the paper's core finding)
> "Crowd-sourced fact-checking provides social media platforms with a promising method of managing misinformation at scale. However, the success of fact-checking programs like X's Community Notes requires the participation of a critical mass of note-writers who have the time and epistemic resources necessary to write and rate high-quality notes... This research identifies how different linguistic communities participate in volunteer fact-checking efforts on X's Community Notes program and addresses volunteers' reliance on professional fact-checking resources differs across languages. We find that while the Community Notes program has had strong uptake in some linguistic communities, the program has failed to catch on in others. Additionally, we confirm findings that **notes that cite professional fact-checkers are considered more helpful, but show that reliance on professional fact-checking overall remains minimal.**"

**Section**: Abstract
**Why it matters**: The bolded finding is a genuine two-sided result the Drafter must present carefully: (a) notes that DO cite professional fact-checkers are rated MORE helpful (supports the "professional fact-checking still adds value even under a crowd-sourced model" leverage claim), BUT (b) overall reliance on professional fact-checking remains minimal (a caveat against overstating the leverage — most Community Notes do not cite professional fact-checkers at all). **Do not cite only half of this finding.**

### Passage 2 — for Q5 (framing context, verbatim)
> "Platforms have pursued two primary strategies for sourcing this human labor: to contract with third-party fact-checkers and to use community-generated fact-checks. Both strategies have important limitations... Professional fact-checkers provide in-depth analyses of complex events, drawing on their own journalistic expertise and resources, including advanced reverse-image search tools and the social capital to seek statements from involved parties, that are often not available to volunteer fact-checkers. However, professional fact-checking struggles to keep up with the sheer volume of misinformation shared online."

**Why it matters**: A clean, citable academic framing of the exact trade-off the brief's Q2/Q5 discussion turns on: professional fact-checkers have expertise and access volunteers lack, but can't scale; crowd-sourcing scales but lacks that expertise. Useful framing sentence for the Drafter's "why platforms want both" context.

**Why it matters**: A specific, citable figure (≥5% of English Community Notes cite professional fact-checkers) — this is the paper's own citation of prior work, not this paper's original finding; attribute to Borenstein et al.
