## Key passages

### Passage 1 — for Q5 (core finding, verbatim, with a directly-quoted fact-checker)
> "Finally, because misinformation has its own life cycle, understanding where a claim is within that cycle would help the fact-checkers better evaluate the future spread of the claim: 'It would have to measure not only the virality, but at which point are we in the virali[z]ation? So if it's starting to viralize, if it's already past its peak point then it's coming down, or if it was already very viral but now isn't anymore.' (P10)"

**Section**: 2.3.5 (prioritization dimensions, virality sub-section)
**Why it matters**: This is the paper's own **direct interview quote** (from an anonymized professional fact-checker, "P10," one of 23 interviewed) describing exactly the "claims have a lifecycle / position-in-cycle matters" argument the brief's Q5 wants support for. This is a stronger anchor than the gate snippet indicated — it's not just the paper's own framing, it's a working fact-checker's own words about why timing/position-in-cycle matters for prioritization. **This is the closest thing in this evidence base to a directly-quotable, peer-reviewed-adjacent source for the brief's "在地感測知識會過期" argument** (label/claim relevance decays as virality position changes) — though note it argues *virality-timing* freshness, which is closely related to but not identical to the brief's own "platforms take a snapshot not a sensor" framing (that specific metaphor is this research's own coinage, not this paper's language — do not present the metaphor itself as sourced from here).

### Passage 2 — for Q5 (local-context value, verbatim — corroborates c020's unverified thesis independently)
> "2.3.6 Tools that better address local context. Many participants expressed concerns about using automated tools for selecting and evaluating claims since most of the contemporary tools do not take local knowledge into account. Fact-checking organizations usually have local experts working on the ground to provide context to understand the misinformation spreading locally."

**Section**: 2.3.6
**Why it matters**: This independently and directly supports the same "local-context knowledge is fact-checking's non-automatable core value" thesis that c020 was cited for but could not be verified (c020 is access_blocked — see c020.md). Since this passage is independently verified here, **the Drafter can make the local-knowledge leverage argument citing c021 alone**, without needing c020 at all.

### Passage 3 — for Q5 (framing/abstract, verbatim — the paper's FABLE framework)
> "Our FABLE Framework of Misinformation Harms incorporates five dimensions of magnitude—(social) Fragmentation, Actionability, Believability, Likelihood of spread, and Exploitativeness—that can help determine the potential urgency of a specific message or claim when considering misinformation as harm."

**Why it matters**: Background context on this paper's actual primary contribution (a harm-assessment prioritization framework, "FABLE"), which is broader than just the lifecycle/local-context points excerpted above — worth knowing the paper's real center of gravity is a structured framework, and the lifecycle/local-context passages are two specific dimensions within a larger interview-based study of 23 professional fact-checkers "from around the world."
