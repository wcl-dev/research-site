# Taiwan Fact-Checking Ecosystem: Agency & Leverage Archive (2024–2026)
*Response to Meta's 2025 third-party fact-checking rollback*

**Context anchor**: Mark Zuckerberg announced Meta would end its U.S. third-party fact-checking program on **January 7, 2025**, replacing it with X-style "Community Notes," and accused fact-checkers of being "too politically biased." *(press, widely reported; e.g. [FEJA 卓越新聞獎基金會, Jan 2025](https://feja.org.tw/78374/))* This was a U.S.-first announcement whose global-partner implications unfolded through 2025.

---

## 1. 台灣事實查核中心 (Taiwan FactCheck Center / TFC)

**Immediate response (~Jan–Feb 2025)** — *press, quoting org directly*
Executive Director 邱家宜 rejected Meta's bias framing and signaled a pivot to domestic funding:
> "Meta過去透過資助全球事實查核組織進行事實查核工作，來證明其有承擔起維護平台訊息品質的責任，如今卻反過來指責事實查核組織有政治偏見、限制言論自由，但此種指控絕非事實。"
> "未來即使國際科技平台斷絕金援，台灣事實查核中心也將努力尋求本土企業與社會大眾的支持，持續我們查核不實訊息、提供真相的工作。"
[TNL 關鍵評論網 (The News Lens)](https://www.thenewslens.com/article/247793)

**Escalation (Dec 2025)** — *press, citing Nikkei Asia (日經亞洲) reporting*
Multiple Taiwan outlets ([ETtoday, Dec 12 2025](https://www.ettoday.net/news/20251212/3083235.htm); [CNA 中央社, Dec 12 2025](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ahel/202512120164.aspx); [GVM 遠見雜誌](https://www.gvm.com.tw/article/126590); [China Times 中時, Dec 12 2025](https://www.chinatimes.com/realtimenews/20251212003862-260408)) reported: Meta funds ~half of TFC's 2025 budget, Google contributes 30%+; TFC's Meta contract may expire **January 2026** with renewal uncertain; TFC is negotiating a new financial arrangement with Meta and simultaneously courting domestic corporate sponsors; as of the report, Meta had not responded.

**Funding diversification — verified history** — *primary, [tfc-taiwan.org.tw/about/donation-report](https://tfc-taiwan.org.tw/about/donation-report)*
Published donor-by-donor figures (last confirmed year: 2024, TWD):
- 2024: Total 2,696,795 — Open Society Institute (FOSI) 2,362,395; small individual gifts.
- 2023: Total 9,152,633 — Open Society Foundations 8,214,250; Meedan 85,853; **Twitter (X) 320,000**; Taipei North Rotary Club 80,000.
- 2022: Total 1,166,836 — Meedan, FOSI, Google GNO 79,423, Rotary Club.
- Stated policy: TFC "rejects government or political donations, no corporate-directed subsidies," relies on foundations/academic grants/competitive programs.
- **Flag**: no 2025 or 2026 donation-report figures were published/found as of this research — the site's transparency page lags roughly a year behind. This means the Dec-2025 Meta/Google percentage claims (from Nikkei via press) cannot yet be cross-checked against TFC's own disclosed ledger.

**New service lines (AI era)** — *primary*
- TFC ran an "Asian Fact-Check Professional Forum" **May 7, 2025**, convening regional teams (incl. Hong Kong) specifically to discuss the fallout of Meta's decision and content-farm disinformation. *(press summary via search; original event listed on TFC channels)*
- TFC 2026 lecture series on **OSINT** (open-source intelligence, May 20 2026) and **AI-forgery detection** ("AI 偽造內容的拆解", July 8 2026) — [tfc-taiwan.org.tw/ai-osint-fact-check-workshop-2026](https://tfc-taiwan.org.tw/ai-osint-fact-check-workshop-2026/).
- TFC expanded into **short-form video / TikTok** in 2025 as a distribution diversification move — reported in TFC's own **"2025 State of Fact-Checking" report** (see below).
- A separate but TFC-linked legal entity, **財團法人新北市台灣事實查核教育基金會 (Taiwan FactCheck Education Foundation)**, runs "台灣媒體素養計畫 2.0" (2024 end–2027 end): high-school media-literacy curriculum, targeting 40+ workshops, 120 courses, 150 teaching materials, "100,000+ beneficiaries," with modules on scam-resistance and AI-content detection — [education.tfc-taiwan.org.tw](https://education.tfc-taiwan.org.tw/) / [school.tfc-taiwan.org.tw](https://school.tfc-taiwan.org.tw/about-media-literacy-program/). **Flag**: funding source for this program (government/foundation) not confirmed in this pass.

**TFC's own "2025 State of Fact-Checking" report** — *primary, [tfc-taiwan.org.tw/2025-fact-checking-report-decline-and-demand](https://tfc-taiwan.org.tw/2025-fact-checking-report-decline-and-demand/)* (published ~Feb 2026, translating/localizing global census data, not Taiwan-specific financials)
- 45.3% of fact-checking orgs globally saw revenue decline in 2025 (vs 15.6% in 2024); ~76% face financial strain; ~32 orgs derive >50% of income from Meta; 8.8% report emergency-level finances.
- Diversification paths cited: membership, training, consulting, research partnerships, academic collaboration, donations.
- Confirms **three IFCN-certified Taiwan orgs**: TFC, MyGoPen, and a third referred to in the WebFetch summary as "Citizens' Truth Commission" — **flag: unverified**, I could not independently confirm this org's Chinese name or existence in a follow-up search; treat as unconfirmed until a primary source names it.
- Argues fact-checking ≠ censorship: "fact-checking supplements information" vs. "content removal obscures it."

**Annual review** — *primary, [tfc-taiwan.org.tw/2025-fact-checking-year-in-review-key-moments](https://tfc-taiwan.org.tw/2025-fact-checking-year-in-review-key-moments/)*: 540+ fact-checks, 85 explainer articles, 200+ IG graphics, 46 videos in 2025 — editorial-output data only; **no Meta/funding content found on this specific page** (contradicts what a search snippet implied — confirmed by direct fetch).

## 2. MyGoPen (麥擱騙)

**Response to Meta's policy shift** — *press*
Project manager 李旭騰 (as cited in search results) advised the public not to take content at face value, to check page-admin info and account creation dates — a "teach self-verification" response rather than a funding appeal. *(Source snippet only — original interview/outlet not directly fetched; treat as press, moderate confidence.)*

**Funding model / independence policy** — *primary, [mygopen.com 贊助辦法](https://www.mygopen.com/p/blog-page_26.html)*
- Explicit rejection of government, party, and politician donations "to maintain operational independence"; all sponsorships publicly disclosed.
- **2025 update, exact quote**: "MyGoPen 目前是透過平台的內容授權以及相關教育推廣的資訊素養專案來支持整體營運，並沒有接受民眾的捐款與贊助" — i.e., **MyGoPen no longer solicits public donations**; it sustains itself via **platform content-licensing deals** and **media-literacy education projects**. This is the clearest documented "new revenue line" in this whole archive — a direct pivot away from donation dependency toward paid licensing/education contracts.
- No explicit statement on Meta or LINE financial relationship was found on this page (flag: gap).

**LINE relationship — active, expanding, government-linked** — *primary, [mygopen.com, published Oct 22 2025](https://www.mygopen.com/2025/10/LINE-Game.html)*
MyGoPen co-launched an anti-fraud LINE chatbot game "逃離詐騙新手村" (Escape Scam Newbie Village) with: **LINE**, 台灣數位安全發展協會 (Taiwan Digital Security Development Association), 台北市電腦商業同業公會 (TCA), **iWIN 網路內容防護機構**, **刑事警察局 CIB** (Criminal Investigation Bureau), and **數位發展部數位產業署** (Ministry of Digital Affairs' Administration for Digital Industries). This is concrete, dated evidence of MyGoPen embedded in a government-and-platform coalition — i.e., its LINE partnership is intact and being actively deepened post-Meta-shock, and it has direct lines into Taiwan state anti-fraud infrastructure.

## 3. Cofacts (真的假的)

**Licensing — correction from initial search**: Two different searches returned conflicting license claims (CC BY-SA 4.0 vs. MIT). Direct fetch of the primary repo resolved it:
- **Primary, [github.com/cofacts/opendata](https://github.com/cofacts/opendata)**: the **opendata repo itself is MIT-licensed**; underlying fact-check content/data is published as a **Hugging Face dataset `Cofacts/line-msg-fact-check-tw`** ("We publish Cofacts data as Hugging Face dataset... For data users, please see [Hugging Face] instead"). The CC BY-SA 4.0 label found in an earlier search snippet likely refers to the content-licensing terms on cofacts.tw itself (I could not confirm this directly — **cofacts.tw/about returned HTTP 403** on fetch, so the "why openness protects us" rationale statement could not be sourced verbatim from the primary page). **Flag: unverifiable at primary-source level this pass** — the CC BY-SA claim rests on a search-engine summary, not a page I fetched myself.

**Rationale for openness — press/secondary paraphrase, not a direct quote**
Multiple secondary sources (Impact Hub Taipei interview, futurecity.cw.com.tw, OCF's own project page) converge on: Cofacts open-sources code and data specifically so that (a) if users distrust Cofacts, they can fork the system and run their own instance, and (b) the data extends beyond Cofacts' own chatbot to power **other Taiwanese anti-fraud/fact-check chatbots**, and its codebase was **exported to Thailand** (Opendream's "Thai Cofact"). **Flag**: this is consistently reported across sources but I did not find a single quotable primary-source sentence stating "openness protects us from platform dependency" — this is my inference from consistent secondary reporting, not a direct organizational claim. Treat as strongly-supported-but-paraphrased, not verbatim.

**Who reuses the data**: confirmed reuse categories are "other Taiwan fact-checking chatbots," "researchers," "investigative journalists," and the Thai fork. **No specific named companies** were identified reusing Cofacts data in this search pass — flag as a gap, not a finding of "no reuse."

## 5. Structural leverage — "platforms still need local fact-checkers" argument

**Named source, primary argument piece**: Wei-Ping Li (TFC research fellow / postdoc, U. Maryland Merrill College of Journalism) & Summer Chen (FactLink co-founder; TFC editor-in-chief 2019–2024), **"The Looming Threat of the Global Disinformation Battle—A Perspective from Taiwan,"** [TechPolicy.Press, Feb 7 2025](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-looming-threat-of-the-global-disinformation-battlea-perspective-from-taiwan/):
- Cites Taiwan's 2020 presidential election, where China-linked and domestic disinformation aimed at voter suppression was identified by fact-checkers, after which "Facebook... significantly curtailed the reach of false information" — direct causal claim that fact-checker input changed platform enforcement.
- Argues the fact-checking "flagging" mechanism disciplines *domestic media* too, "motivat[ing] Taiwanese news outlets to be more mindful of verification."
- Warns Meta's retreat "may dismantle the safeguards... particularly at this time when malicious actors, such as authoritarian regimes, are exploiting the free media environment in democratic countries" — i.e., an authoritarian-interference-vector argument specific to Taiwan's geopolitical position.
- **Does NOT address** label-freshness, new-scam detection, or EU DSA spillover — I searched for this separately (see below) and found no author making that specific composite argument for Taiwan.

**EU DSA angle — checked, weak/no Taiwan connection found**: DSA enforcement news (EU Commission's Oct 28, 2025 preliminary finding that Meta/TikTok breached transparency obligations, incl. researcher-data-access failures) is real and dated, but **legal commentary explicitly states the DSA does not obligate third-party fact-checking as a moderation tool** — meaning the "DSA forces global consistency" leverage argument is weaker than the brief hypothesized. **No source found linking DSA enforcement to Taiwan/Asia policy spillover** — flag this sub-argument as evidenced-absent, not confirmed.

**Public-demand evidence (a different kind of leverage — reputational/political, not contractual)**: "2025假訊息認知調查報告," published **May 18, 2026** via [TNL](https://www.thenewslens.com/article/267704), conducted by **台灣韌性社會研究中心** and **臺大新聞所** (press, third-party academic survey, NOT TFC's own poll):
- 96.52% of respondents had encountered false information (up from 75% in 2022).
- **94%** support "應該/非常應該" legislating platform accountability.
- 76% believe platforms should proactively limit false-information spread.
- 33% admit judging information truth by "情緒好惡" (emotional reaction) rather than verification — cited as evidence that lay self-verification is unreliable, implicitly justifying continued need for professional fact-checking.
