### Three-line say-do contrast

The 2018 register (Lyons) explicitly conceded Meta *needs* fact-checkers because it "won't be able to address this problem with human fact-checkers alone" and structured its own detection systems to "make the most of each rating from fact-checking partners" — i.e., partner labor was an acknowledged input to Meta's ML pipeline, not a favor Meta was doing the checkers. The 2025 register (Kaplan/Zuckerberg) recasts the same apparatus retroactively as inherently biased and censorious ("a tool to censor," "too politically biased"), collapsing the earlier "we rely on their expertise" framing into "they destroyed more trust than they created" — with no acknowledgment that Meta's own newsroom had spent 2016–2021 crediting that expertise as load-bearing. The Taiwan case (Chen Yi-Ju, 2020) shows the courtship register was localized and personal ("we're delighted," "look forward to partnering with more local organizations") — a texture the January 2025 US-only announcement doesn't reckon with at all, since the exit statements speak in the register of a US "politically biased" problem while the global/Taiwan partnerships were pitched as expanding trust-building infrastructure.
